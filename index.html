About

I am a PhD student in Complex Systems and Data Science at the University of Vermont. I work in the Joint Lab with Jean-Gabriel Young and Laurent Hébert-Dufresne.

Before coming to Vermont, I studied liberal arts at St. John’s College, worked at MITRE, and worked on the Coherent CAPTAIN-Mills dark matter experiment at Los Alamos National Laboratory.

In summer 2026, I was a Data Science for Social Good fellow at Johns Hopkins, working with the City of Baton Rouge on early warning models for blight and structural deterioration.

Research

I study how to infer the rules of a networked system from what it does. This includes reconstructing networks and contagion processes from observations, improving inference on graphs with loops, and exploring methods for Bayesian learning that avoid sampling. I also study how language models make choices when their preferences are inconsistent.

My work brings together network science, Bayesian inference, and machine learning. A full list of papers is on the publications page.
